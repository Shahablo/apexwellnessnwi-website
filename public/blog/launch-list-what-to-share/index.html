A launch list needs your email, not your medical history

An email signup has one job. It should not need a plot summary of your medical history.

At Apex Wellness, the Launch List is for opening updates. It asks for your name and email. A question about your own health deserves a different, private conversation, not a public comment or an extra paragraph in a signup box.

By Apex Wellness Editorial Team · Published September 27, 2026

In this article

  • Match the information to the purpose
  • Keep the question without sending it yet
  • Read what you are agreeing to
  • Where to find Apex updates

Match the information to the purpose

Before filling out a form, look at what it says it is for. Joining an update list, asking an administrative question, and completing a form provided for care are different tasks.

The Apex Launch List asks for your name and email. Leave symptoms, medications, test results and medical history out of it. Do not put those details in a social comment or message instead.

Keep the question without sending it yet

A signup form being the wrong place for a question does not make the question unimportant. Keep a private note somewhere you normally store your own information. Two headings can help:

  • Practical details to find: location, published fees, contact routes or how a practice describes its services.
  • Questions for an individual conversation: anything that depends on your circumstances and belongs with a qualified clinician.

This is about where to have the conversation, not whether it is worth having. The note can be short. No one is grading the appendix.

Read what you are agreeing to

Read the consent statement and linked privacy information before joining a list. Check that you entered an email address you actually use, and do not submit someone else’s contact details without permission.

Receiving opening updates is not the same as completing a medical intake. If a detail you need is not on the page, keep the question open rather than assuming the signup answers it.
