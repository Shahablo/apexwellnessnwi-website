Before You Choose a Wellness Practice: Questions Worth Asking

You can read five clinic websites and still not know three basic things: who you would actually see, what you would pay, and where a question goes after the first visit.

Those details deserve as much attention as a practice's introduction. A short list of questions gives you something concrete to compare, especially when two websites use nearly the same language.

The prompts below are about how a practice operates. They do not tell you whether a treatment is right for you. Ask them of us, too.

By Apex Wellness editorial team · Published September 21, 2026

Start with responsibility

Ask who would conduct the first evaluation, who would review any results, and who would make decisions about your care. Write down the person's role as well as the name. A team photo or the phrase "physician-led" does not tell you which person handles each step.

Then ask how decisions get explained. Where can you raise a question, and who would talk it through with you? You are looking for a clear account of who is responsible for what. A list of credentials is not the same thing.

Ask what the practice does and does not provide

A service name can cover a wide range of arrangements. Ask the practice to explain its scope in ordinary language, including which concerns it does not manage and what happens when a request falls outside that scope.

If you already see another clinician, ask how information could be shared with your permission. Do not assume a specialty service replaces an existing care relationship, or that every practice coordinates records the same way.

Put the fee beside what it includes

Ask for an itemized explanation of the quoted price. Which visits or services are included? Could testing, medication, outside services or follow-up carry separate charges? Are there recurring payments, and what are the terms for stopping them?

For weight-loss programs specifically, the National Institute of Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney Diseases recommends asking about total costs, additional charges, staff training and how the program operates. That guidance supports asking practical questions. It does not vouch for any particular clinic.

Find out what happens afterward

Before relying on a phrase like "ongoing support," ask what it includes. Which contact route is meant for routine questions? Who receives them? What response times does the practice publish? How are follow-up visits arranged?

Ask separately how to request your records or have them sent elsewhere. Read the practice's privacy information before using an online form. A marketing inquiry form and a medical intake form have different jobs.

Keep one small comparison sheet

Copy these lines into a private note and leave room for each practice's answer.

  • Decision maker and role: ______
  • Services included and outside scope: ______
  • Quoted fee, inclusions and possible additional charges: ______
  • Follow-up and routine-question arrangements: ______
  • Records request and transfer process: ______
  • Answer received from, date, and question still open: ______

Write "not yet answered" where you need to. A blank should stay visible until you get a usable answer. Save a link or written explanation with the date, so you can tell later whether the information changed.
