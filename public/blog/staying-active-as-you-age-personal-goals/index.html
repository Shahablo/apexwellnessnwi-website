What Does Staying Active Mean to You?

"I want to stay active" can mean a Saturday on the golf course, an afternoon tending plants, a favorite class, or time out with friends. It can also mean continuing to take part in something you have loved for years, with whatever arrangements make participation possible.

The phrase is personal. You do not have to turn it into a race time, a gym goal or a version of someone else's life to make it worth discussing.

This short exercise is a way to describe what matters to you. It is not an exercise plan or a way to decide what is medically suitable.

By Apex Wellness editorial team · Published September 20, 2026

In this article

  • Name the part that matters

Start with a scene

Think of an ordinary moment you would like to keep in your week, or one you would like to return to. Picture the setting, the people and the part you enjoy.

Perhaps it is working on a garden alongside a neighbor. Perhaps it is joining a family outing, attending a dance class, or spending time at a place you enjoy. You might value the conversation as much as the activity itself.

Write one sentence: "I would like more room in my life for ______ because ______."

There is no need to choose something impressive. A clear, familiar example tells another person more than a broad statement about becoming your "best self."

Name the part that matters

Two people can name the same activity for very different reasons. One may enjoy learning a skill. Another may value company, time outdoors, a break from work, or keeping a family tradition.

Add a second sentence: "The part I most want to keep is ______."

This can reveal flexibility in the goal. If the important part of an outing is being with friends, you can talk about the arrangements around that outing without treating a particular pace or route as the entire point. That observation is about preference, not a judgment about anyone's ability.

Describe the week you actually have

Consider the practical details: work hours, caregiving, transport, available time, the cost of the activity, and whether you prefer company or solitude. Write down the details that matter to this particular idea.

Use neutral language. "My evenings are usually taken" is information. It does not need a second sentence criticizing your discipline. If a health question affects your plans, keep it separate for a private conversation with a qualified clinician; this worksheet does not answer it.

Make a note you can use

Copy these prompts into a notebook or a private document. A few words in each space are enough.

  • An activity or experience I value: ______
  • What I enjoy about it, including who I share it with: ______
  • What participation would mean to me: ______
  • Practical arrangements I would need to consider: ______
  • What I would like another person to understand: ______
  • A question I want to bring to an appropriate professional: ______

You can leave a line blank. You can also change your answer later. The purpose is to give your own priorities some detail, not to grade yourself or commit to a program.

Keep the definition yours

At Apex, interest in strength and activity is part of the story behind the team. People who do not identify as athletes belong in that conversation too. You do not need to borrow the team's hobbies to explain what matters in your own life.
